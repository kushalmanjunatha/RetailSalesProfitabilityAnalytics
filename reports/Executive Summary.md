# Retail Sales and Profitability — Executive Summary

**Project:** Retail Sales, Pricing & Profitability Analytics  
**Period:** 2023–2025 | **Sample:** 18,240 illustrative orders | **Currency:** USD

## Business question

Which product categories, sales channels and discount patterns deserve attention when balancing retail revenue and profitability?

## Results at a glance

| Key metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Orders analysed | 18,240 |
| Net revenue | US$14.45 million |
| Profit | US$3.31 million |
| Overall profit margin | 22.90% |
| Loss-making orders | 784 (4.30%) |

## Main findings

1. **Beverages:** Highest category revenue (US$3.74 million) but lowest category profit margin (14.02%). It also accounts for 510 of the 784 loss-making orders.
2. **Sales channels:** Wholesale achieved a 25.90% observed margin versus 12.41% for Online, highlighting an area for deeper cost and product-mix investigation.
3. **Discount patterns:** The 10–15% discount range had a 23.42% observed margin, but this is a descriptive association, not evidence that larger discounts improve profit.
4. **Decision support:** Excel and MySQL summaries, Python visualisations and a three-page Power BI report were used to explore business KPIs and segmentation.

## Suggested business actions

- Investigate loss-making Beverages orders at product level before changing prices.
- Compare Online and Wholesale profitability after controlling for product mix and costs.
- Use the dashboard to monitor revenue, profit, margin and loss-making orders together.

## Scope and limitations

The dataset is illustrative, not verified transactions from an Australian retailer; all financial figures are in USD. Recommendations require testing with operational data before use in a real business.
